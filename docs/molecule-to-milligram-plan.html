Molecule to Milligram

A staged plan to digitize the path from a generated structure to a validated compound, and the audit that says where to start.

Scope  KRAS G12C · G12D · G12V · G13D Audit set  73 generated + 2 reference drugs Prepared  31 August 2026
01

Why a year was lost

The synthesis work did not stall because the lab was slow, or because reagents in Nigeria were impure. It stalled because the molecules handed to the chemists cannot exist.

Three rows in the cross-docking output are labelled for the bench: Hit 13, Hit 41, and Hit 73. All three carry the same three groups in one structure.

Row sent to the chemists Structure Groups present
Hit 13 — “Molecule 2 for synthesis” NN(N1)C2CCC[C@H1]2CCCC3OC31CC=O triazane, epoxide, aldehyde
Hit 41 — “molecule for synthesis” NN(N)C1CCC[C@H1]1CCCC2OC2C=O triazane, epoxide, aldehyde
Hit 73 — “molecule 1 for Synthesis” NN(N)C1CCC[C@H1]1CCCC2OC2CN(OCC3CCCCCCCC=4)CC=43 triazane, epoxide, N–O
The chemistry

A triazane — three nitrogens bonded in a row — is not an isolable organic functional group under ordinary conditions. Beyond that, the hydrazine end and the aldehyde react with each other on contact to form a hydrazone, and the hydrazine also opens the epoxide. Each of these molecules attacks itself.

No degree of skill, equipment, or reagent purity produces a compound that is not stable against its own functional groups. The professors were given an impossible assignment and spent a year proving it.

This is not confined to the three chosen rows. Running the full set through RDKit against the two approved KRAS drugs in the same sheet:

The alert census across the 73: hydrazine N–N in 44, triazane N–N–N in 34, N-oxide or nitroso in 19, peroxide O–O in 15, epoxide in 13, strained alkene in 11, aziridine in 5, diketone in 4, azo in 3, boron in 2. Your own ADMET column already predicted a median mutagenicity of 0.93 across the set. Nothing in the pipeline acted on it — PAINS and epoxide were written into the report as fields to read, never as filters that stop a molecule.

A second check asks whether a molecule’s own groups react with each other. Fifteen do, and all three rows marked for synthesis are among them. This is a cheap test — two substructure matches and a lookup table of incompatible pairs — and it alone would have caught the year.

None of the 73 reach the bench. That is the honest read, and it is worth more than the shortlist was, because it names the defect precisely: the generator was never taught what a real molecule looks like, and nothing downstream checked.

The scoring function also fails its own control

Sotorasib, an approved G12C inhibitor, scored −5.00 against G12C — worse than 68 of the 73 generated molecules. Adagrasib reached −9.12, and four generated molecules beat it. A ranking that places a clinical drug near the bottom of its own target list is not yet evidence of anything.

Part of this is structural: both drugs are covalent inhibitors that form a bond to the mutant cysteine, and neither DiffDock nor GNINA models covalent bonding. Cross-docking them against G12D, G12V and G13D compounds the problem, since those variants have no Cys12 for the warhead to reach. The controls need to be non-covalent binders before the numbers mean anything.

02

The system

Ten stages, each a service with its own API and interface, each useful alone, composed by an orchestrator into one pipeline.

That split is the product decision. A chemist anywhere should be able to paste one structure into stage 03 and get a verdict without adopting anything else — that is the wedge, and the thing your own program most needed. The full chain is what you run internally and what an enterprise buys.

01

Target intake

Built

Disease, UniProt ID, PDB structure, sequence, variant set, and known reference drugs registered as one record that every later stage reads.

Alone
A target registry with structure and variant provenance.
Have
The FastAPI request model already carries all of it.
02

Structure generation

Needs retraining

The conditional VAE ensemble, four checkpoints across the KL sweep, sampled at four temperatures. It works mechanically. It produces molecules outside drug-like space because nothing in the objective pulled it toward chemistry that exists.

Alone
Candidate generation for a protein target.
Fix
Constrain to reaction-accessible space, or generate fragments and assemble by known reactions, so every output has a route by construction.
03

Chemical reality gate

Missing — build first

The stage whose absence cost the year. Sanitization and valence, the PAINS, BRENK and NIH alert catalogs, an instability set for triazanes, peroxides, aziridines and strained alkenes, and a pairwise check for groups that cannot coexist in one molecule — a hydrazine with an aldehyde, an epoxide with a free amine. Verdict plus reasons, not a score.

Alone
Paste a structure, get pass or fail and exactly which groups conflict. The most shippable standalone product in the stack.
Build on
RDKit FilterCatalog plus a curated SMARTS set. Days of work, not months.
04

Route and buyability

Missing

Retrosynthesis returns a route tree or nothing. Forward reaction prediction checks each proposed step actually gives the intended product. Starting materials are matched against live supplier catalogs, with cost and lead time attached. “No route” becomes a hard stop rather than a conversation with a professor.

Alone
Can this be made, by what route, from what, at what price, delivered where.
Build on
AiZynthFinder or ASKCOS, a forward reaction transformer, and the Enamine, Mcule and Sigma catalog APIs.
05

Binding prediction

Built, uncalibrated

The DiffDock and GNINA service already runs. What it lacks is any demonstration that its ranking separates binders from non-binders on this target.

Alone
Docking as a service. It already is one.
Fix
Known actives and property-matched decoys per target, a published enrichment figure, MM-GBSA rescoring on the survivors, and covalent docking wherever a warhead is present.
06

ADMET and toxicity

Reports, does not gate

The ADMET panel is computed and written into the report. A molecule with predicted mutagenicity of 0.93 still ranked highly, because these values were never wired to a threshold.

Alone
Property and liability profile for any structure.
Fix
Thresholds that block, with the blocking reason carried into the report.
07

Potency prediction

Present, not predictive

The current model returns one of ten wide IC50 bands from sequence, structure and three descriptors. Across a bucket range that wide, trained on one ligand per protein, it approximates the training prior rather than the target.

Alone
Potency estimate with a calibrated confidence interval.
Fix
Rebuild against measured assay data per target family, and report uncertainty. This only becomes real once stage 09 returns data.
08

Cell and tissue response

Missing

Predicted cell-line sensitivity, assay outcome, and morphological profile — the in vitro digitization layer. Starts from public pharmacogenomic and imaging corpora, and is only worth trusting on your chemistry once your own assays feed it.

Alone
Which cell lines to run, and what to expect, before booking bench time.
Honest limit
Literature-trained on day one. Useful for prioritizing, not for claiming activity.
09

Exposure and dose

Missing

Physiologically based pharmacokinetic simulation for absorption, distribution, exposure and organ burden, plus predicted study design. This is how in vivo work gets shorter and smaller, not skipped.

Alone
Dose and exposure projection for a candidate.
Build on
PK-Sim and the Open Systems Pharmacology suite, which are open source.
10

Lab execution and capture

Missing — the real unblock

Orders placed with a contract lab from inside the system, compound and batch tracking, and ingestion of what comes back — NMR, LC-MS, HPLC purity, assay readouts — attached to the molecule record that produced them. This is the layer that turns physical results into training data instead of email attachments.

Alone
A lab notebook that knows which prediction each result tests.
Why it matters
Every stage above improves only if measured outcomes come back. Without this, the platform never learns.
03

Sequencing

Ordered so that each phase produces something defensible on its own, and so the earliest work is the work that was missing.

Weeks 1–4

Close the gate

Build stage 03 and run it back over all four KRAS result sets, not just the cross-docked 75. Ship it as a standalone checker at the same time.

ProducesA shortlist that survives chemical scrutiny, or a documented zero — and either is a real answer for a clinical partner.

Weeks 4–12

Make it orderable

Add stage 04. In parallel, screen a purchasable make-on-demand library against the four variants and order the top hits from a supplier who ships with purity data.

ProducesPhysical, QC‑verified compound in hand without solving a synthesis route.

Months 3–6

Calibrate and capture

Enrichment benchmarks for stage 05, thresholds for stage 06, and stage 10 standing up so assay results land against the molecules that predicted them.

ProducesFirst measured binding data, and a scoring function with a known hit rate.

Months 6–12

Close the loop

Stages 07 to 09 retrained on returning data, with the generator retrained under synthesizability constraints and each cycle selecting the next batch.

ProducesA pipeline that improves per cycle rather than per rewrite.

04

The parallel track

Buy the first compounds instead of making them.

Make-on-demand catalogs hold billions of compounds that have never been synthesized but are built from validated reactions and stocked building blocks. Success rates run above eight in ten, delivery is three to four weeks, and each compound arrives with purity data attached.

Screening that space against your four KRAS structures and ordering the top hits gets you real assay data in about a month, from compounds a supplier is contractually obliged to deliver pure. It removes both blockers at once — the synthesis route and the reagent quality — and it does not depend on any of the platform work above.

The generator keeps its role as the source of novel structures and the basis of any composition-of-matter claim. It just should not be what stands between you and your first measurement.

On the reagent problem

Impure starting material is a supply chain fault, not a modelling one, and no amount of software fixes it. A contract lab with purity obligations and analytical certificates removes it, and will very likely cost less than another year of faculty time.

05

What stays wet

Worth stating plainly, because the plan is credible only if its boundary is honest.

  • The first binding measurement. Docking confidence and a predicted potency band are hypotheses. a clinical partner asked for laboratory validation because that is what evidence of activity means, and they were right to.
  • Animal studies. Simulation shapes the study — dose, schedule, which endpoints, how many animals — and can shrink it considerably. It does not replace it, and no regulator currently accepts that it does.
  • Analytical confirmation. Structure and purity of a physical sample are measured, never predicted.

Digitization here is about making the wet lab rare and precise rather than removing it. The target is not zero experiments. It is that every experiment you run tests a molecule that could exist, has a route, and has cleared everything a computer can decide on its own.
